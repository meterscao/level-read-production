# Source Research

Research happens before drafting. A short title or summary is a lead, not sufficient evidence for all details in a finished news item.

## With a source URL

Open and read the supplied article first. Extract the event, people or institutions involved, date and place, figures with units, attribution, context, and any conditions or uncertainty. If the page is unavailable, search for the exact headline, named people, organisation, place, and date; use a credible version of the original reporting or a primary source where possible.

## Without a source URL

Search actively before drafting. Begin with the distinctive names, place, event, and time period in the user's brief. Prefer primary documents, official statements, original reporting, and established news organisations. Use more than one source when a contested, consequential, or numerical claim needs corroboration.

## Fact brief and limits

Before writing, establish the core event: who did what, when and where it happened, and why it matters. Record the source links used and separate:

- confirmed facts;
- attributed claims (including who made the claim);
- conditions, caveats, and unresolved details.

Write only what the sources support. Preserve attribution and uncertainty in all three levels. If the core event cannot be verified, ask the user for a source or narrower facts instead of inventing details. For deliberate `manual-input` stories, use only the supplied facts and say that external verification was not available.
